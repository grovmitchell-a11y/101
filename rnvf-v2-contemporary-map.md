# Regional Net Value Framework v2: the contemporary map

## Instructions to the rater

You are the assessor for a universal sovereign. Picture a Renaissance pope over independent princes who can and do fight each other: the sovereign draws revenue from every region on earth, keeps the peace between the princes, and needs the system's flows of goods, energy, money, people and knowledge to keep moving. You will rate one region's net value to that sovereign, as it stands in the current decade, on a scale of 1 to 10.

Return the deliverable in the format at the end. Do not recommend policy. Do not forecast beyond 2027. Do not grade on a curve.

REGION TO ASSESS: [name and code]

## Time lens

- Anchor on 2025, the last complete year. Use 2021 to 2027 only to decide what is structural and what is a one-off shock. Count structural changes in full: a war in its third year, a closed mine, a depopulating district. Ignore one-off shocks: a flood year, a price spike, a pandemic year.
- Count only what is operating. Fabs under construction, announced cities, canals on paper and planned bases earn nothing until they run.
- For spending and transfer patterns use 2019 or 2023 to 2025. Never use 2020 or 2021, when pandemic transfers made nearly every region on earth a net recipient.
- This is not a peak-ever rating. Rate the region as it is, with the people it has.

## Unit

Rate the region as given, not its country. Where a statistic exists only at national level, say so, and explain why it applies to this region before using it. Never paint a region with a national number silently. Regions in the same country must differ when their assets differ.

## The scale

5 is neutral: the region neither costs nor benefits the sovereign once everything is counted. Above 5 is net value, below 5 is net cost, and distance from 5 is magnitude. The scale is global and absolute. One ruler rates Gaza, Mississippi, rural Niger and Tokyo, and the anchors below fix it. No number has to be rare and no number has to be common.

| Tier | Meaning | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Loss would force a crisis response from the very top, cost a major expedition to reverse, and leave lasting damage if unreversed | Carthage to the Western Empire; Tokyo Bay; Hormuz's shores; Hsinchu |
| 9 | Loss would force a treaty-level response and a permanent program to compensate, but would be absorbed | Nisibis in 363; London on its ledger alone; Hampton Roads |
| 8 | A large net benefit, still common; loss is noticed in the accounts, not a crisis | Spain to Rome; any +2 asset region |
| 7 | Moderate net benefit; matters in mass; losing one is not a systemic event | Most productive regions of the rich world |
| 6 | Slight net benefit, or a deficit rescued by a modest asset | Britannia |
| 5 | Breakeven; also empty land that costs and yields nothing | Interior Antarctica; a subsistence region |
| 4 | Minor deficit; costs upkeep, yields little | Rural Bihar; Finnish Lapland; eastern Poland |
| 3 | Clear deficit, painful in mass | Mississippi's rural regions; subsidized Bavarian and Italian districts |
| 2 | Large deficit, or a liability that outweighs real assets | A retirement coast; a capital's ledger before its identity row |
| 1 | Catastrophe: the sovereign pays heavily to hold it and gains nothing by holding | Dacia; Gaza; Donbas |

## Step 1: the ledger sets the default tier

The sovereign's question: after the region's people consume what they consume, and after the sovereign pays for everything it runs there, is there a surplus left, and how big?

Ledger = output, minus household consumption, minus public spending that lands in the region, in a normal year, in billions of dollars.

Rules:
- Revenue is the region's output, not tax actually collected. Collection reflects tax policy, not the region.
- Public output is valued at cost in GDP. Count a capital's bureaucracy, a garrison, a base and a federal payroll on the spending side only.
- Costs count where they land: pensions, healthcare, subsidies, military cost, security operations, disaster relief, all of it, whatever the reason it is there. An old population consumes more than it produces, and that is counted.
- Investment is surplus the region keeps. It counts as surplus.
- No floor. A region nobody spends on and nobody taxes sits near zero.
- Where fiscal data does not exist, estimate from output per head, consumption level, and the national public-spending share, adjusted for capitals, bases, subsidy programs and retiree concentrations. Say which figures are estimates.

Bands, surplus or deficit in billions of dollars per year:
10: +100 and up. 9: +40 to +100. 8: +15 to +40. 7: +5 to +15. 6: +1 to +5. 5: between -1 and +1. 4: -1 to -5. 3: -5 to -15. 2: -15 to -40. 1: -40 and below.

## Step 2: the eight asset rows

Each row asks the same three questions about its own kind of asset:
1. What share of the system's supply or capacity is this?
2. How substitutable is it, and from where?
3. How many years to replace it if it were lost?

Score the row: +3 means sole or near-sole source with a generation to replace, and it is a 10 on that row. +2 means a quarter or more of world supply, or a two-or-three-supplier oligopoly, with years to replace, and it is an 8. +1 means a leading producer in a substitutable category, and it is a 6 or 7. Nothing means write "none".

Three rules for every row:
- Count only value to the rest of the system beyond this region's own output. A port that handles its own hinterland's trade is already in GDP and earns nothing here.
- Name the plant, the port, the lab, the base, the strait, the mine. Never the country. Reputation, nationality and fame are not evidence.
- Answer every row for every region. Write "none" where there is nothing. Do not skip rows.

**Economic weight.** Two parts. Scale is share of world GDP: 10 at 0.5 percent and up; 9 from 0.2 to 0.5; 8 from 0.08 to 0.2. Below 0.08 percent write none and let the ledger decide: gross size without surplus is not an asset to the sovereign, and only a behemoth matters by its size alone. Irreplaceable output: apply the three questions to the region's most important manufactured goods and services. Anchors: +3 Veldhoven (every EUV lithography machine), Oberkochen (every EUV mirror), Hsinchu and Tainan (leading-edge logic), Japan's photoresist clusters. +2 Kyoto's passive components, Toulouse and Everett for large aircraft, Tuttlingen's surgical instruments. +1 German and Japanese machine-tool regions, Basel pharma, Jubail and Antwerp petrochemicals, Ulsan shipbuilding. Machine tools as a category are +1 or +2, never a jump to 10.

**Resources.** Checklist, look for each: energy (oil, gas, coal, uranium); metals (iron, copper, bauxite, nickel, cobalt, lithium, rare earths, platinum group, chromium, manganese, niobium, tin, zinc); industrial minerals (phosphate, potash, boron, helium); grain and oilseeds; tropical crops; livestock and fish; water sources; forest. Rate current structural production, not reserves. A country's share is not a region's share. Anchors: +3 Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province oil, Katanga cobalt, Ganzhou heavy rare earths, the Bushveld's platinum, Araxá niobium, Yamal gas. +2 Pilbara iron, Atacama lithium, Sulawesi nickel, Saskatchewan potash, Boké bauxite, Ras Laffan gas, Mato Grosso soy, the Mekong Delta's rice, Tibet's water towers. +1 a Corn Belt region, a palm-oil region, a Permian region. The Corn Belt test: would anyone actually go without, or just pay more?

**Infrastructure.** Nodes the system's flows pass through with no quick alternative: ports, canals, locks, pipelines, cable landings, grain and LNG terminals, refineries, hubs, dams, grids. Test: if this node closed for a year, what would the rest of the system lose beyond this region's own output? Anchors: +3 Shanghai-Ningbo, Singapore, Rotterdam-Antwerp with the Rhine, Ras Laffan, Houston's refining belt, Egypt's cable corridor, Suez, Panama. +2 the Soo Locks, Duisburg, Novorossiysk, Yanbu and Fujairah as Hormuz bypasses, Northern Virginia's data centers, New Orleans grain, Marseille's cable landings, Odesa's grain terminals. +1 any national hub with a regional alternative. Atlanta's airport is the busiest on earth and is +1, because every flight reroutes. The Soo Locks are small and +2, because nearly all of America's iron ore passes through them.

**Research.** Standing capacity to produce knowledge the system depends on and could not rebuild within a generation. Test: if this region's institutions and firms stopped, which fields would slow, by how much, and for how long? Prestige, the count of universities and historical contribution are not evidence. Most research is corporate, so Suwon, Dongguan and Toyota City count beside universities. Anchors: +3 the Bay Area, Boston, Beijing's Haidian, Cambridge, Oxford, Leuven for semiconductors, Zurich and Geneva. +2 Munich, Paris-Saclay, Tokyo, Seoul and Daejeon, Hsinchu, Shenzhen, Tel Aviv, Toronto, Grenoble, Bangalore. +1 a flagship university or national lab with replaceable output.

**Military.** Capacity the system could not quickly rebuild: yards, arsenals, nuclear infrastructure, launch sites, command nodes, recruiting grounds. Test: if this capacity vanished, what share of the system's ability to deter, project or police goes with it, and how long to rebuild? Every prince's capacity counts the same way: Severodvinsk and Dalian weigh as Newport News does. A base is both position and capacity, so give the larger of this row and the strategic row, not the sum. Defense industry is already in output; this row is for single-source capacity. Anchors: +3 Hampton Roads, Puget Sound, the Kola bastion, Hainan, Brest, Faslane, the main launch sites. +2 Ramstein, Al Udeid, Yokosuka, Camp Humphreys, Severodvinsk, Dalian, Jiangnan, Barrow, Kiel, Portsmouth.

Recruitment is scored on this row. In a volunteer-force country, a region with a documented, disproportionate supply of soldiers is +1; a major recruiting ground is +2; the recruiting heartland of a great power's army is +3. The row needs a young population; an aged region cannot be a recruiting ground whatever its tradition. In conscription countries write none. Anchors: +3 Pakistan's Salt Range Punjab. +2 Nepal's Gurkha hills, India's Punjab and Haryana, the US South's base belt, Buryatia and Dagestan under current conditions. +1 Scotland and the North East of England, Fiji, the Pashtun belt, Kurdistan, rural Georgia, the Mezzogiorno for Italy's forces.

**Strategic position.** Control, not output: how much the system's flows and stability depend on this region staying in reliable, solvent hands. Test: if it fell into hostile or incompetent hands tomorrow, what stops moving, how much of the world's flow is that, and how fast can it be rerouted? Contested places score on the answer, not on the contest. Defensible is a cost, not a value; the cost of holding belongs to the ledger. Anchors: +3 Hormuz's shores, Malacca, Suez and Sinai, the Bosphorus, Panama, both shores of the Taiwan Strait. +2 Bab-el-Mandeb, Gibraltar, the Danish straits, Okinawa, Guam, Djibouti, the GIUK gap, Iceland. +1 a held, stable frontier that shields a core. Deflation: Crimea is +1, since the Black Sea balance and Odesa's grain both have workarounds. Taiwan's strategic row is 8; its 10 comes from chips.

**Identity.** Political, religious and civilizational: does legitimacy, administration, or a civilization's identity run through here? Anchors: +3 Washington, Beijing, Moscow, Delhi, Rome with the Vatican, Mecca, Jerusalem, Istanbul. +2 other great-power and major-power capitals, Athens, Kyoto, Varanasi, Qom, Najaf. +1 the capital of a mid-size state, a major pilgrimage or cultural center. A ruin with no living continuity earns nothing. The capital of a failed or hostile state earns little here and a liability below.

**Environment.** Natural systems the region's governance actively protects and the system depends on: water towers, aquifers, carbon sinks under deforestation pressure, major fisheries. Anchors: +3 Tibet's water towers, scored once, here or under resources. +2 the Ethiopian highlands as the Nile's source, the Amazon basin regions in mass, the Congo basin, the Great Lakes. +1 boreal forest regions, a major upwelling fishery. An ice sheet nobody manages earns nothing. Hazards go under liabilities.

## Step 3: liabilities

One row, subtracted in step 4. A cost already in the ledger is not a liability again: the garrison a frontier costs and the threat it faces are two different things.
- Active war zone: -3.
- Actively threatened frontier, meaning shooting, raids, insurgency, or a garrison that dominates the region's ledger: -2. A latent claim is 0.
- Ungoverned space exporting disorder to neighbors: -2.
- Hostile or illegitimate control with no functioning administration: -2.
- Hazard to neighbors, such as a volcano, a failing dam, a nuclear plant in a war zone, a flood source: -1 or -2.

Shadow economies, informality, housing costs, peaceful separatist movements, youth unemployment and low trust are not liabilities. They are features of the ledger, or nothing.

## Step 4: aggregation

Final tier = the highest tier among the ledger and the eight rows, minus liabilities. Not a sum, not an average. Add +1 if two rows besides the best one sit at 8 or above. Floor 1, cap 10.

Why: a sum averages the vital organs away. Dhahran is a 10 on resources and a 5 on everything else, and it is a 10. A capital is a 2 on the ledger and a 10 on identity, and it is a 10. A region with a 3 on the ledger and none everywhere else is a 3.

Check against the Roman anchors. Rome around 400: ledger 2, identity 10, no liabilities, final 10. Britannia: ledger 3, strategic 8 because it denies a raid base against Gaul, threatened frontier -2, final 6. Dacia: ledger 2 from gold minus the garrison, nothing else, threatened frontier -2, final 1.

## Step 5: the discipline clause

The final tier is a judgment, and every judgment is written down. Thresholds set the default. You may move a region only for a reason you can state in one sentence that names an output, a node, an asset or a liability, its share of the system, and the time to replace it. Reputation, nationality, fame, news coverage and "everyone knows" are not reasons.

## Deflation anchors

Famous places that score below their reputation. Treat the tests as literal.
- The Arctic is not a 10. Northern Norway is 7 or 8 for Barents gas and NATO's flank. Norrbotten is 7 for Kiruna. Finnish Lapland is 4 or 5.
- Aberdeen is not a 2. It is a 7: the highest output per head in Britain outside London, a surplus region on the ledger, with a resources +1 for the North Sea fields.
- Dublin is not a 7. It is an 8 or 9, depending on how much of its GDP you discount as multinational accounting.
- Eastern Poland is not a 1. It is a 4.
- Northwest Bulgaria, Molise, the Irish border regions and Western Macedonia are 3 or 4, not 1. Peacetime Europe has no 1s.
- Crimea is a 3: strategic +1, ledger 2, war zone -3.
- Odesa is a 5: infrastructure +2, ledger 2, war zone -3.
- Taiwan is a 10 for Hsinchu and Tainan. Its strategic row is 8.
- Oxford and Cambridge are 10 on research. Manchester is not.
- Campania is a 7: scale 8, Vesuvius and the Campi Flegrei -1. It is not a 3.
- Germany does not get a 10 for being Germany. Munich and Frankfurt reach 10 with named irreplaceable output. Most German regions are 8 or 9.
- Washington is a 10 on identity and a 2 on the ledger. Both are written down.
- A Florida retirement coast is a 2 or 3. That is harsh and correct.
- Rural Niger is a 5 on the ledger and a 3 or 4 after the Sahel's liabilities. Rural Bihar is a 4.
- Interior Antarctica is a 5.
