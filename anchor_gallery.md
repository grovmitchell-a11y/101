**PEAK WINDOW + moment:** Window 7, High medieval, **c. 1000–1020 CE** (pre-Qarakhanid-war, pre-Mongol florescence). Contested with Window 4 (c. 110 BCE, Dayuan) and Window 11 (c. 1817, Kokand khanate) — three-way ambiguity reported per procedure.

**WINNING PATHWAY:** GDP (pop share × argued 1.2–1.3 commercial multiplier, Kashgar-road belt). Population pathway nearly ties.

**LAWYERED SHARE:** GDP **0.13–0.18%** of world; population **0.11–0.14%** (c. 1000–1020). Co-contenders: c. 110 BCE pop 0.06–0.12%; c. 1817 GDP 0.09–0.14% (Ruling A multiplier on khanate fiscal documentation).

**BAND:** **10** (lawyered, top-of-range per Ruling I; all three candidate windows clear 0.09% at their tops, so the band survives the collapse of any two). Consensus band: **8–9**, straddling the 0.05% line.

### Numerator components (with citations)
- **c. 1000–1020:** the 10th-c. geographic corpus (al-Istakhri, Ibn Hawqal, al-Muqaddasi; Hudud al-'Alam) credits Farghana with on the order of forty minbar-towns — one of the densest town rosters in Islamdom, endorsed as exceptional by Barthold (*Turkestan Down to the Mongol Invasion*). Valley construction: ~40 minbar-towns at high-range 10–25k each + rural hinterland at 2.5–3 persons per irrigated ha over the attested medieval canal command → **0.95–1.2M valley**; polygon allocation 30% (Quba ranked by the geographers just behind the capital Akhsikath; Margilan, Rishtan, Khavakand/Khuqand all attested 10th-c.; the southern fans are the valley's largest) → **285–360k**. Multiplier 1.2–1.3 ("argued" commercial tier): the Kashgar road ran through the polygon (Khuqand–Margilan–Quba–Osh–Irkeshtam), with attested sericulture (Margilan) and ceramics (Rishtan).
- **c. 110 BCE (Dayuan):** Shiji 123 — some seventy walled towns, population "several tens of myriads" (數十萬), maximal reading ~400–500k; Hanshu 96A as usually read: 60,000 households, 300,000 persons, 60,000 bowmen. Valley 300–500k × 30% polygon → **90–150k**. Multiplier 1.0–1.2 (heavenly-horse export to Han argued) → GDP 0.06–0.13%.
- **c. 1817 (Kokand khanate, Umar Khan r. c. 1810–1822):** polygon ~450–550k (Kokand city ~80k per 19th-c. visitors, anchored by the 1897 census figure of ~82k; Margilan ~30k; dense irrigated countryside, 1897 backcast). Multiplier 2.0–2.5 under the "quantified fiscal/commercial documentation" tier, named sources: the Kokand khanate archive (Troitskaya's 1968 catalogue); the Qing–Kokand trade corpus (Fletcher in *Cambridge History of China* vol. 10 — the 1832 settlement as China's "first unequal treaty"; Newby, *The Empire and the Khanate*, 2005). Kokand farmed customs on the Altishahr trade and ran the Russia–Kashgar transit. GDP **0.09–0.14%**.
